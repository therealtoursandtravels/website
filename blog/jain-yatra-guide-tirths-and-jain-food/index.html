Jain Yatra Guide: Major Tirths and How We Serve Pure Jain Food on Tour

By The Real Tours and Travels · Mulund West, Mumbai · 23 November 2026

For many Jain families, a tirth yatra is the most meaningful journey of the year. The biggest worry is usually food – finding pure Jain meals on the road. That is why we travel with our own Maharaj. Here is a short guide to the most-loved yatras and how we plan them.

Popular Jain tirth yatras

Palitana (Shatrunjaya), Gujarat

Shatrunjaya hill at Palitana is one of the holiest Jain tirths, with hundreds of temples on the hilltop reached by a long climb of steps. Most yatris start the climb early in the morning. For seniors, doli (palanquin) services are available at the foothill.

Girnar, Gujarat

Girnar near Junagadh is sacred to Bhagwan Neminath. The climb is long, so we plan rest stops and an early start. It combines well with Palitana in one Saurashtra yatra.

Sammed Shikharji, Jharkhand

Sammed Shikharji (Parasnath hill) is where twenty of the twenty-four Tirthankaras attained nirvana, making it one of the most important pilgrimages for Jains. The parikrama is a long trek, usually started before dawn.

Ranakpur and Rajasthan tirths

The Ranakpur temple is famous for its marble work and its many carved pillars – no two are said to be alike. A Rajasthan yatra can also include Dilwara temples at Mount Abu, Nakoda and other tirths.

Shravanabelagola, Karnataka

Home of the monolithic statue of Bhagwan Bahubali (Gommateshwara), Shravanabelagola is a peaceful yatra that can be combined with other South India tirths.

How we take care of Jain food

  • Our own Maharaj travels with the group and cooks fresh, pure Jain food – no onion, garlic, potatoes or other root vegetables.
  • Chauvihar-friendly timings – dinner is planned before sunset wherever the itinerary allows.
  • Tithi requirements respected – tell us about green vegetables, ekasana or ayambil needs and we plan the menu around them.
  • Boiled water arrangements on request.

Planning tips for a comfortable yatra

  • Choose the right season – winter months are the most comfortable for hill climbs.
  • For elderly yatris, we arrange doli services, rest days and ground-floor rooms where possible.
  • Carry light cotton clothing for the temple, comfortable footwear for the approach, and a small torch for early climbs.
  • Group yatras are a great way for families and sanghs to travel together – we handle transport, stay, food and darshan timings.

Frequently asked questions

Do you provide pure Jain food on yatra tours?

Yes. Our own Maharaj travels with the group and cooks fresh Jain food without onion, garlic or root vegetables, with chauvihar-friendly timings.

Which Jain tirths can be covered in one trip from Mumbai?

Palitana and Girnar are often combined in one Gujarat yatra. Ranakpur, Mount Abu and Nakoda make a good Rajasthan yatra. Sammed Shikharji is usually a dedicated trip.

Can elderly family members do the Palitana climb?

Yes, with planning. Doli services are available at the foothill, and we schedule rest days and early starts.
